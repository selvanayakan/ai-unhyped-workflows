# Chapter 2 scoring checklist v2 (fixed 15 Oct 2026, before re-scoring)

Every number in the video comes from this checklist. Each item is a yes/no, so anyone can check it against the answer.

## A. Covers every step (count out of 10)
A step counts if it appears anywhere in the answer's "current process" section (as a step, or explicitly flagged there as unclear).
1. US sellers ship stock to the warehouse
2. Orders arrive by WhatsApp, emailed spreadsheet, and phone calls (local shops)
3. Office staff retype everything into one Excel sheet every morning
4. Drivers get a printed list at 9 am
5. Drivers call customers to confirm address and time
6. If the customer doesn't pick up, the driver comes back with the parcel
7. The sheet is only updated in the evening
8. US sellers asking "where is my order" wait (until night / a full day, time difference)
9. Month-end: the accountant bills each seller from the Excel sheet, USD for US sellers and INR for local shops
10. Tally is part of their tools (mentioned, or its role flagged as unknown)

## B. Pain points tied to the email (count out of 5)
A pain point counts if (a) its evidence is something the email actually says and (b) it adds no cause or effect the email doesn't state.

## C. Testable user stories
- Number of stories: is it 6-8? (yes/no)
- Acceptance criteria: count how many are testable (a tester can mark pass/fail from something observable, with no undefined words like "quickly", "clearly", "real-time", "efficiently") out of the total.

## D. Realistic automation (count out of 3)
An idea counts if all three are true: (a) it names a specific tool or product; (b) it builds on what they already use (WhatsApp, Gmail, Excel, Tally) instead of replacing it with enterprise software; (c) its cost is stated within "a few hundred dollars a month" for 40 people, or the named tool has a public plan under about $100/month at this size.

## E. Invented facts (count; lower is better)
A claim stated as fact, outside the assumptions section, that the email does not say. Each one is quoted.
- Not counted: simple arithmetic on the email's numbers (40 - 6 = ~34 drivers); anything in a labelled assumptions section; future-state proposals clearly labelled as proposals.
- Counted: invented numbers, causes, roles, systems, timings, or what a tool is used for.

## Our verdict
The counts are facts anyone can check. Which answer is "better" is **our opinion**, said clearly as our opinion, weighing the counts above and Selva's view as a business analyst.
